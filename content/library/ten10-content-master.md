Scripture says 'the whole,' for it could have said 'your yield.' What does 'whole' come to include? Interest, trade, and anything a person gains from...

"כבד את ה׳ מהונך ומראשית כל תבואתך". שב אל עניין הביטחון, ואמר "כבד את ה׳ מהונך", ובא להזהיר שלא תרע עינו בפזרו ממון למצוות ולצדקות, ולא ידאג פן יחסר הונו במתנת ידו, אבל יבטח בה׳ כי בגלל הדבר הזה יברכהו וכל אשר לו ירבה...

והנה זה מדרכי הביטחון, שיבטח לבו כי כאשר יחסר ממונו למצווה, כן ירבה וכן יפרוץ. ואמרו ז״ל: "מלח ממון חסר". קיום הממון ומליחתו — על ידי החסרון.

**content_en:**

'Honor the Lord from your wealth, and from the first of all your produce.' This returns to the subject of trust. He said, 'Honor the Lord from your wealth,' and he comes to warn that one's eye should not turn bad when scattering money for commandments and for gifts of charity, and that one should not worry lest his wealth shrink because of the gift of his hand. Rather, he should trust in the Lord, for on account of this thing He will bless him, and all that is his will increase...

This, then, is one of the ways of trust: that his heart trusts that when his money is reduced for a commandment, so it will multiply and so it will break outward. And the sages of blessed memory said: 'Salt for money is reduction.' The money's lasting, and its salting, comes through the reduction.

## TN10-0008

**id:** `TN10-0008`

**source:** על פי הספר "נפלאים מעשיך", לבעל בן איש חי, פרק קנ״ה  
**source_en:** Draft for review. Not a published translation. Following the book Nifla'im Ma'asekha, by the author of Ben Ish Chai, chapter 155.
**text_status:** `adapted_story`  
**verification_status:** `not_checked`  
**original_location:** שורות 14–16

**content:**

מעשה שהיה בראובן ושמעון, שהיו שכנים. בתחילה לא היו עשירים, ולאחר מכן התעשרו, אך הסיבה לעושרם הייתה שונה.

ראובן שמע חכם שדורש את מאמר הגמרא: "אוקירו לנשייכו כי היכי דתתעתרו" — כלומר, כבדו את נשותיכם כדי שתתעשרו. הוא האמין בכל לבו שאם הבעל מכבד את אשתו במלבושים נאים ובתכשיטים יקרים, יתעשר. הוא התאמץ מאוד, כיבד וייקר אותה תמיד — והתעשר עושר גדול.

שמעון, שכנו, שמע מאותו חכם את מאמר הגמרא: "עשר תעשר — עשר בשביל שתתעשר". הוא האמין בדבר באמונה שלמה, והפריש מעשר לצדקה מכל נכסיו ומכל רווחיו. אף הוא התעשר עושר גדול.

כשראה שמעון שגם ראובן התעשר, אף שלא ראה אותו נותן צדקה מרובה, שאל אותו על כך. כאשר שמע שהעושר בא בעקבות הכבוד שנהג באשתו, הצטער על שלא שמע גם על סגולה זו. הסכומים שהוציא לצדקה היו גדולים פי ארבעה ממה שהוציא שכנו על אשתו; נוסף על כך, שכנו נהנה מן ההוצאות והחפצים נשארו ברשותו, ואילו מכספי הצדקה נהנו העניים בלבד.

הלך שמעון אל החכם והתרעם: מדוע לא סיפר על שתי הסגולות יחד, כדי שכל אדם יוכל לבחור ביניהן?

השיב לו החכם: דוד המלך כבר נתן לך תשובה ניצחת: "אל תירא כי יעשר איש, כי ירבה כבוד ביתו. כי לא במותו יקח הכל, לא ירד אחריו כבודו". "בית" זו אישה, שנקראת בית. המכבד אותה זוכה לעושר, כפי שזכה שכנך. אבל אל תקנא בו, כי באמת חלקך גדול מחלקו. אף שהוצאותיו נשארו ברשותו והוא גם נהנה מהן, במותו לא ייקח עמו את ההנאה ואת הכבוד. ואילו אתה, מלבד העושר בעולם הזה, תקבל על המעשר שכר נצחי רב בעולם הבא.

**content_en:**

A thing that happened with Reuven and Shimon, who were neighbors. At first they were not rich, and afterward they grew rich, but the cause of their wealth was not the same.

Reuven heard a sage expounding the saying of the Talmud: 'Honor your wives, so that you become wealthy' — that is, honor your wives in order that you grow rich. He believed with his whole heart that if a husband honors his wife with fine clothing and costly jewels, he will become rich. He made a great effort, and he honored her and held her dear always — and he grew rich with a great wealth.

Shimon, his neighbor, heard from that same sage the saying of the Talmud: 'Tithe, you shall tithe — tithe so that you become wealthy.' He believed the matter with a complete faith, and he set aside a tithe for charity from all his property and from all his profits. He too grew rich with a great wealth.

When Shimon saw that Reuven had also grown rich, although he had not seen him give charity in any large amount, he asked him about it. When he heard that the wealth had come in the wake of the honor he showed his wife, he was grieved that he had not also heard of this practice. The sums he had spent on charity were four times what his neighbor had spent on his wife. Beyond that, his neighbor enjoyed the outlays, and the objects remained in his possession, whereas from the charity money only the poor had any benefit.

Shimon went to the sage and protested: why did you not speak of the two practices together, so that each person could choose between them?

The sage answered him: King David already gave you a decisive reply. 'Do not be afraid when a man grows rich, when the honor of his house increases. For at his death he will not take everything; his honor will not go down after him.' 'House' — that is a wife, who is called a house. One who honors her merits wealth, as your neighbor merited. But do not envy him, for in truth your share is greater than his share. Even though his expenses remained in his possession, and he also enjoyed them, at his death he will not take the enjoyment and the honor with him. You, though, besides the wealth in this world, will receive for the tithe a great and lasting reward in the world to come.

הצדיק רבי שלום אהרן לופס זצ״ל, מהעיר חאלב שבסוריה, ששימש בארץ כרבה של עכו, היה ידוע בעיסוקו בצדקה באופן על־טבעי כל ימיו, נוסף על גדלותו בתורה.

אנשים היו פונים אליו כמנהל קרן צדקה. פעם שאל בחיוך: "איזו קרן אני מנהל? הם אינם יודעים שהקרן היחידה שיש לי היא זו שנאמר עליה: 'והקרן קיימת לו לעולם הבא'".

**content_en:**

The righteous Rabbi Shalom Aharon Lofes, of blessed memory, from the city of Aleppo in Syria, who served in the Land of Israel as the rabbi of Acre, was known for his occupation with charity in a manner beyond the ordinary, all the days of his life, besides his greatness in Torah.

Already in his cramped home in the Beit Yisrael neighborhood, poor people and the broken-hearted lived there as a regular thing, and he shared his bread with them. He distributed enormous sums, a large part of which came from his personal salary as rabbi of the city.

They said of him: 'Rabbi Lofes lives on a tithe.' What they meant was that more than once, instead of setting aside only a tenth for charity, only a tenth of his salary remained in his hand for his most basic and necessary needs, and all the rest he scattered to the poor and the downtrodden.

People would turn to him as the manager of a charity fund. Once he asked, with a smile: 'Which fund am I managing? They do not know that the only fund I have is the one of which it is said: and the principal remains for him for the world to come.'

שמונה מעלות יש בצדקה זו למעלה מזו:

א] מעלה הגדולה שאין למעלה ממנה, המחזיק ביד ישראל המך ונותן לו מתנה או הלואה, או עושה שותפות או ממציא לו מלאכה [וכן אם קונה אצלו וכדומה] כדי לחזק ידו שלא יצטרך לבריות ולא ישאל, ועל זה נאמר: ״והחזקת בו״.

ב] פחות מזה הנותן צדקה לעני ולא ידע למי יתן, ולא ידע העני ממי מקבל. וקרוב לזה, הנותן לקופה של צדקה. ולא יתן אדם לקופה של צדקה אלא אם כן יודע שהממונה עליה נאמן ויודע לנהוג בה כשורה.

ג] פחות מזה, שיודע הנותן למי נתן ולא ידע העני ממי לקח. כגון גדולי החכמים שהיו הולכים בסתר ומשליכים המעות בפתחי העניים, וכזה ראוי לעשות, ומעלה טובה היא, אם אין הממונים על הצדקה נוהגים כשורה.

ד] פחות מזה שיודע העני ממי נוטל, ולא ידע הנותן למי נתן, כגון החכמים שהיו צוררים המעות בסדיניהם ומשליכים אותם לאחוריהם ובאים העניים ונוטלין כדי שלא יהיה להם בושה.

ה] פחות מזה, שיתן לעני לידו קודם שישאל.

ו] פחות מזה, שיתן לו כראוי אחר שישאל.

ז] פחות מזה, שיתן לו פחות מהראוי בסבר פנים יפות.

ח] פחות מזה שיתן לו בעצב.

**content_en:**

There are eight levels in charity, each one above the other:

1] The greatest level, and there is none higher than it: one who takes hold of a poor Israelite and gives him a gift or a loan, or makes a partnership with him, or finds him work [and likewise if he buys from him, and the like], in order to strengthen his hand so that he will not need other people and will not have to ask. Of this it is said: 'And you shall strengthen him.'

2] Below this: one who gives charity to a poor person, and the giver does not know to whom he gives, and the poor person does not know from whom he receives. Close to this is one who gives to the charity chest. A person should not give to the charity chest unless he knows that the one in charge of it is trustworthy, and that he knows how to handle it properly.

3] Below this: the giver knows to whom he gave, and the poor person does not know from whom he took. For example, the great sages, who would go secretly and throw the coins at the doorways of the poor. It is fitting to act this way, and it is a good level, if those in charge of the charity are not conducting it properly.

4] Below this: the poor person knows from whom he takes, and the giver does not know to whom he gave. For example, the sages who would bundle the coins in their sheets and throw them behind themselves, and the poor come and take them, so that they would have no shame.

5] Below this: he gives to the poor person into his hand before the poor person asks.

6] Below this: he gives him what is fitting, after he has asked.

7] Below this: he gives him less than what is fitting, but with a pleasant face.

8] Below this: he gives to him in sadness.

But in his youth he happened to come to the town of Slonim, and there he merited to take part, for two Sabbaths, at the pure tables of the author of Divrei Shmuel, of blessed memory. He was deeply moved by the personality of the Rebbe, and also by the songs and the atmosphere of holiness that filled the place. He decided that this was his place, and he planned to remain in Slonim and to attach himself to the Rebbe. But on the third Sabbath he saw a way of conducting himself, in the Rebbe, that changed his mind. In the middle of his pure table, and at the height of the lifting-up of the soul in which he was held, the Rebbe called the gabbai and asked in a whisper — which was heard quite well — how is my money? Are you a good keeper? Are you sure you locked the cupboard? Please go and check again... The young Rav Kahaneman was in shock. How is it possible, at a time lifted up like this, when everyone here is immersed in an atmosphere of the upper worlds, to think of such things — and on the Sabbath besides! He changed his mind and decided that the place was not for him...

Decades later he once met the Rebbe of Slonim, Rabbi Shalom Noach Berezovsky, of blessed memory, author of Netivot Shalom, who told him of the holy ways of the Divrei Shmuel. He kept inquiring and pressing all the time whether they were being guarded as they should be, and he always warned the gabbaim not to grow lax in the guarding. Even in the middle of the tish he would ask them about it. Only now did Rav Kahaneman grasp what a terrible mistake he had made when he judged the Rebbe without justice. He told the whole story to Rabbi Shalom Noach and sighed: 'What a shame. I could have stayed with the Divrei Shalom. I enjoyed it so much there, and I felt an enormous benefit...' The Rebbe, who saw his great sorrow, comforted him that surely all of it had been from Heaven. 'There are enough Slonim Hasidim,' he said, 'but there is only one Ponevezher Rav. It seems they wanted, from Heaven, that you raise up the world of Torah anew after the terrible destruction.' And so he calmed him with his words, until his mind was set at rest.

And our rabbis, of blessed memory, said (Taanit 9a) that in the commandment of the tithes alone was the examination permitted, as it is said: 'Bring the whole of the tithe into the storehouse, and let there be provision in My house, and try Me, please, in this.'

And Solomon, peace be upon him, said (Proverbs 3:9–11): 'Honor the Lord from your wealth, and from the first of all your produce.' Your granaries will be filled with plenty, and your vats will burst outward with new wine. 'The Lord's discipline, my son, do not reject, and do not grow sick of His rebuke.' The explanation: if it does not turn out so for you, that your granaries are filled with plenty, when you give of your wealth and of the first of all your produce for the work of charity, but days of poverty seize you, do not reject the Lord's discipline. And know that this too is for your good. 'For whomever the Lord loves, He corrects' (there, verse 12) — so as to do him good at his end, that in place of the reward of this world, and in place of its honor, there be a true reward, and the good laid up in store, which stands for all time.

Nor did I have parents who would assist me, for Father at once sent me to Mother, so that she would send me back to Father, and so around again.

I did what life had taught me best. I prayed. Blessed be the Lord, I received a temporary post, filling another's place, at a low wage, but better than nothing. One of my sisters-in-law [my husband's sister], a righteous woman named Miriam, to whose heart my situation came very near, encouraged me greatly and gave me strength to go on coping. By the mercy of Heaven we merited to be remembered with a sweet baby girl, who brought much light into the house, but not a few difficulties. Miriam truly went beyond her bounds, and with a giving of herself she helped in everything she could. She in effect served as my mother. She even came with me to the hospital, to support, to help, and to offer of her experience to a young mother. My parents came to visit and to wish mazal tov, each on a separate day, and each also promised that the other side would bring the gift. When the time came to do the shopping in honor of my sweet little angel, Miriam took me to a costly shop and truly pleaded with me to choose from the best. I had always been used to expensive brands, and now too I could not take my eyes off a costly carriage that I had always dreamed of. Miriam took the carriage, and many other items of good quality, went up to the counter, and paid. There was no one happier than I, and I thanked her from the depths of my heart. She said that this was from her monetary tithe, and that I had nothing to feel ill at ease about.

I knew that Miriam had an especially high salary. A few months earlier she had moved up to a truly good position, and the pay matched it. But it could not be accepted that this was a tithe and nothing more. My husband too was stunned, and he too held that the sum she had spent on us had gone past the tithes.

A month later Miriam invited me and my husband to an evening meal at their house. We came. Miriam and her husband set out a table in the manner of a king, abundance in truth. It was pleasant and agreeable, and then they told us why they had really invited us. Miriam's husband had always dreamed of setting a fifth of the income aside for charity, and of not making do with a tithe alone, but Miriam found this hard. Each time there was another reason why not, and it was always a justified reason as well. But after large and unexpected expenses fell on them over some months, Miriam was persuaded to double the tithe and to set a fifth aside, despite the difficulty, and from then on they saw only blessing. At once I remembered that Miriam had received the remarkable position only a number of months before, and she confirmed to me that a month after she set a fifth aside for the first time, she received an offer of work without having searched. All this good, and all this plenty, she received just so. She only opened her hands. Since then they are very careful to set a fifth aside for charity, without clever evasions. They told that for several months they had put the money of the fifth aside, in order to help me after the birth. After everything I had been through, they so much wanted to make me a happy mother.

They added that there is an old and wise saying in Chinese: 'Give me a fish, and I eat today; teach me to fish, and I eat all my life.' And this is what they decided to do, to teach us to fish... We said we would think it over. Meanwhile we had almost no income. After the leave following the birth, I had no post to return to. After much weighing of it we decided to make the effort, and to try to set a fifth aside from the little that we have. It was not easy in the least. And besides, the time was the eve of Pesach, and the expenses were many. My husband received a special stipend, which also included kimcha de-Pischa. We decided to set a fifth aside from this sum as well. I do not know how, but for the festival I managed to buy a garment for myself and for the baby, exactly as I had wanted. But that is not all.

During Chol ha-Moed my husband met one of his friends, at a gathering arranged by the graduates of the yeshiva in which he had studied. That man told him that he had advanced to be head of a field in a large high-technology company. My husband asked him whether he was looking for a worker, and he nodded vigorously, and wondered how he knew. My husband told him about me, and about the temporary post I had held. In short, every requirement they set out was met. He put me forward to the head of the team, a woman observant of Torah and the commandments, who understands the matter, so that in every respect it was more than fitting for me. I passed the interview and the tests successfully. I almost could not believe it, but I already had somewhere to return after the leave following the birth.

Since then I say to any woman who is willing to hear: Give, and tithe. You will see that you grow rich.

**content_en:**

**content_en:**

Once there lived a great baal nefesh, exacting in the commandments, who would not put into his mouth anything he had not produced himself in his own house, from bread through to noodles, and even fruits and vegetables. And every need of a commandment he grew himself, so as not to make use of anything ready-made from outside. They said of him that even for the gifts to the paupers he did not rely on all the organizations and the various functionaries, who come up with paupers in honor of Purim. This precious Jew would produce his own paupers himself, for the need of the commandment. And how did he do it? At the new moon of Adar he would bring two poor men into his house, feed them scant bread, and give them tight-measured water to drink, until they reached a state in which they crave every thing, according to the halakhic explanation: 'a pauper — one who craves every thing.' All the while he would inquire and investigate and make certain that they had no property of their own, nor any side income, and that they did not receive any stipend either, and so on. At the height of the day of Purim he would give each of them, in fear and in love, a sum of money that counts as a 'gift,' and send them home — not before he had stipulated with them that they return the next year, at the new moon of Adar. May his merit protect us, and from him let them learn, and so let them do.
